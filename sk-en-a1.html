Section 0How to use this course

Slovak is one of the friendliest Slavic languages for a beginner: it is read as it is written, it has no articles, and stress is always on the first syllable. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, directions, shopping, time and everyday matters.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. No silent letters. Stress is always on the first syllable.
  • No articles. kniha = book, the book, a book.
  • Words you know: pivo, taxi, hotel, banka, doktor, metro, študent, telefón, pizza.
  • No "r with zh" sound. Slovak has no Czech ř, so it is easier to pronounce.
What will be new. Nouns, adjectives and pronouns change their endings (six cases). At A1 you only need the nominative, accusative and a few set patterns. Learn words inside short phrases, not alone.
Main tip. Learn phrases: not "piť = to drink", but "pijem kávu = I drink coffee". The endings then come by themselves.
False friends and traps. pozor = attention, pokoj = peace (a room is izba), hodina = hour, lesson, pravda = truth, chudobný = poor, čerstvý = fresh, zima = winter and cold, pomoc = help, nemoc = illness.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 12.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so rely on the audio. "kh" is the Scottish loch, "aa, ee, oo" mark long vowels. Slovak stress is always on the first syllable, so it is not marked.

Section 1Sounds and alphabet

Slovak spelling is nearly phonetic. The difficulty is a few accent marks and some less common letters (ä, ô, ľ, dz, dž).

Soft letters before i and e

Before i, í, e, é, ä, ia, ie, iu the letters d, t, n, l are pronounced soft (dy, ty, ny, ly): deti [DYE-tyi], ticho, nie [nye]. Exceptions are mostly foreign words.

The rhythmic law. Two long syllables do not follow each other: krásny (beautiful) but pekný, and dobrý but pekná. You only need to know that this exists, as you will meet it in adjectives.

1.3. Stress and syllables

  • Stress is always on the first syllable: BRA-ti-sla-va, DYA-ku-yem, SLO-ven-sky.
  • One-syllable prepositions join the next word: v Bratislave is said as one word.
  • r and l can act as vowels: prst (finger), vlk (wolf), krk (neck), Brno; they may also be long: ŕ, ĺ (rare).

Section 3Nouns, gender and plurals

Slovak has three genders: masculine m (blue), feminine f (red) and neuter n (green). There are no articles. Gender is usually seen from the ending.

3.1. Gender from the ending

3.3. Nominative and accusative

Accusative (the direct object)

Feminine -a becomes -u: Mám knihu. Vidím ženu. Masculine animate nouns take -a: Vidím pána. Mám brata. Masculine inanimate and neuter stay the same: Mám stôl. Vidím mesto.

Negation

Add ne- to the verb: som → nie som (for "to be", nie is written separately), mám → nemám. Slovak uses a double negative: Nemám nič. (I have nothing), Nikto tu nie je. (Nobody is here).

The main verb stays in the infinitive: Môžem vojsť? Chcem piť kávu. Musím pracovať. For a learned skill ("can swim") use vedieť: Viem plávať.

Môžem vojsť? | mwozhem voysty | May I come in? Chcem piť kávu. | khtsem pity kaavu | I want to drink coffee. Dnes musím pracovať. | dnes museem pratsovaty | I have to work today. Lekár: Ako dlho? | For how long? Pacient: Dva dni. | Two days. Lekár: Berte tieto tabletky a zostaňte doma. | Take these tablets and stay at home.
