Section 0How to use this course

Czech looks fearsome on paper, but it is read exactly as it is written, it has no articles, and the pronunciation is very regular. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, directions, shopping, time and everyday matters.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. No silent letters. Stress is always on the first syllable.
  • No articles. kniha = book, the book, a book.
  • Words you know: pivo, taxi, hotel, banka, doktor, metro, student, telefon, pizza.
  • Short, reliable verb patterns. One present tense, no "-ing" forms.
What will be new. Nouns, adjectives and pronouns change their endings (seven cases). At A1 you only need the nominative, accusative and a few set patterns. Czech is "ending-heavy", so learn words inside short phrases, not alone.
Main tip. Learn phrases: not "pít = to drink", but "piju kávu = I drink coffee". The endings then come by themselves.
False friends and traps. pozor = attention, nemoc = illness, pomoc = help, hodina = hour, lesson, pravda = truth, chudý = poor, úžasný = amazing, čerstvý = fresh, pokoj = room and peace, zima = winter and cold.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 12.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so rely on the audio. "kh" is the Scottish loch, "rzh" is the special Czech ř (see section 1), "aa, ee, oo" mark long vowels. Czech stress is always on the first syllable, so it is not marked.

Section 1Sounds and alphabet

Czech spelling is nearly phonetic. The difficulty is a few accent marks and one famous letter, ř.

1.1. Vowels: short and long

Length matters: byt (flat) and být (to be) differ only in length. Long vowels have an acute accent (´) or a ring (ů). Just hold them about twice as long.

The ř problem. Start with "r" and "zh" said quickly together. Even Czechs spend years learning it as children. At first "rzh" and "rsh" are fine and you will be understood. Practise: tři (three), řeka (river), dobře (well), třicet (thirty).

1.3. Stress and syllables

  • Stress is always on the first syllable: PRA-ha, DYE-ku-yi, CHES-ky.
  • One-syllable prepositions join the next word: v Praze is said as one word, "VPRA-zhe".
  • Long vowels do not move the stress.
  • r and l can act as vowels: prst (finger), vlk (wolf), krk (neck), Brno, čtvrtek (Thursday).

Section 3Nouns, gender and plurals

Czech has three genders: masculine m (blue), feminine f (red) and neuter n (green). There are no articles. Gender is usually seen from the ending.

3.1. Gender from the ending

3.3. Nominative and accusative

Accusative (the direct object)

Feminine -a becomes -u: Mám knihu. Vidím ženu. Masculine animate nouns take -a: Vidím pána. Mám bratra. Masculine inanimate and neuter stay the same: Mám stůl. Vidím město.

Negation

Add ne- to the verb: jsem → nejsem, mám → nemám. Czech uses a double negative: Nemám nic. (I have nothing), Nikdo tu není. (Nobody is here).
