Section 0How to use this course

German has a reputation for being hard, but it is very logical: you pronounce what you see, and the rules are clear. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, the city, shopping and time.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. Learn a few rules for ei, ie, eu, ch, sch and the umlauts.
  • Words you already know: Hotel, Familie, Bank, Telefon, Musik, Student, Apfel, Hand, Finger.
  • All nouns are capitalised.
  • Three genders: der (masculine), die (feminine), das (neuter). Learn each noun with its article.
  • Word order: the verb is always in second position in a simple sentence.
False friends. Gift = poison, bald = soon, Rock = skirt, also = so, therefore, Art = kind, type, Chef = boss, Kind = child, Fabrik = factory, Brief = letter, Rat = advice, Bad = bath, Handy = mobile phone, bekommen = to get.
Politeness. du is for friends, family and children. Sie (capital S) is for strangers and formal situations. If in doubt, use Sie.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 9.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so treat it as a hint and rely on the audio. "kh" is the sound in Scottish loch, "ue" is like French tu (say "ee" with rounded lips), "uh" is a weak vowel like the a in about.

Section 3Nouns, gender, plurals and articles

Three genders: masculine der (blue), feminine die (red), neuter das (green). Gender often does not match English logic (das Mädchen "the girl" is neuter), so learn every noun with its article.

3.1. Articles

Definite (the)Indefinite (a)
mder Mannein Mann
fdie Fraueine Frau
ndas Kindein Kind
pl.die Kinder— (no article)
Negation: kein and nicht

kein negates a noun: Ich habe keinen Bruder. nicht negates a verb or adjective: Ich arbeite nicht. Das ist nicht gut.

Accusative

Only the masculine changes: der → den, ein → einen. Feminine, neuter and plural stay the same: Ich sehe den Mann, die Frau, das Kind. The accusative marks the direct object (what or whom you see, have, buy).

The verb frame

The modal is in 2nd position and the main verb goes to the end as an infinitive: Ich kann heute nicht kommen. Ich muss arbeiten. Also: sollen (should), möchten (would like).
