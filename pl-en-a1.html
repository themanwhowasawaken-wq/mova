Section 0How to use this course

Polish looks intimidating, with its consonant clusters and seven cases, but it is regular, it has no articles, the stress is nearly always on the second-to-last syllable, and the spelling is a reliable guide to the sound. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, directions, shopping, time and everyday matters.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. Once you know the letter combinations (sz, cz, rz, ch), you can read anything aloud.
  • No articles. książka = book, the book, a book.
  • Words you know: bank, hotel, taksówka, pizza, telefon, student, metro, kawa, herbata.
  • Stress is predictable. Almost always on the second-to-last syllable: POL-ska, dzię-KU-ję.
What will be new. Nouns, adjectives and pronouns change their endings (seven cases), and verbs agree with the subject. At A1 you only need the nominative, accusative and a few set patterns. Learn words inside short phrases, not alone.
Main tip. Learn phrases: not "pić = to drink", but "piję kawę = I drink coffee". The endings then come by themselves.
False friends. dres = tracksuit (not dress), gruby = fat, thick (not grubby), list = letter, szef = boss, sympatyczny = nice, aktualny = current (not actual), ewentualnie = possibly (not eventually), magazyn = warehouse (and magazine), pensja = salary, kiosk = newsstand.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 12.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so rely on the audio. "kh" is the Scottish loch, "zh" is the s in measure, "on / en" mark the Polish nasal vowels ą and ę. Stress is almost always on the second-to-last syllable, so it is not marked.

Soft consonants with "i"

When i comes after c, s, z, n and before another vowel, it only softens the consonant and is not pronounced separately: nie [nye], siostra [SHOS-tra], ciasto [CHAS-to]. Before a consonant or at the end it is a normal "ee": ci, si, zi, ni.

Don't panic about the consonant clusters. szcz = "shch" as in fresh cheese: szczęście (happiness). Read clusters slowly letter by letter, then speed up. Practise: dziękuję, trzy, chrząszcz.

1.3. Stress and syllables

  • Stress is almost always on the second-to-last syllable: POL-ski, uni-wer-SY-tet (a few foreign words are exceptions).
  • Every vowel is a syllable, and the final consonant of a word is devoiced like in German: chleb is "khlep".
  • Short prepositions join the next word: w Polsce sounds like one word.

Section 3Nouns, gender and plurals

Polish has three genders: masculine m (blue), feminine f (red) and neuter n (green). There are no articles. Gender is usually seen from the ending.

3.3. Nominative and accusative

Accusative (the direct object)

Negation

Put nie before the verb: Nie jestem. Nie mam. Polish uses double negatives: Nic nie mam. (I have nothing), Nikt tu nie jest. After a negated verb the object goes into the genitive: Mam czas → Nie mam czasu.

Modal + infinitive

The main verb stays in the infinitive: Mogę wejść? Chcę pić kawę. Muszę pracować. "Can" in the sense of a learned skill is umieć: Umiem pływać.

Czy mogę wejść? | chi mogeh veyshch | May I come in? Chcę pić kawę. | khtseh peech kaveh | I want to drink coffee. Dzisiaj muszę pracować. | dzeeshay moosheh pratsovach | I have to work today. Teraz nie mogę rozmawiać. | teraz nye mogeh rozmavyach | I can't talk now. Umiesz gotować? | oomyesh gotovach | Can you cook? Section 11Four more dialogues

# 1. U lekarza (at the doctor's) Lekarz: Dzień dobry, co panu dolega? | Good day, what's troubling you? Pacjent: Boli mnie głowa i mam gorączkę. | I have a headache and a fever. Lekarz: Jak długo? | For how long? Pacjent: Dwa dni. | Two days. Lekarz: Proszę brać te tabletki i zostać w domu. | Take these tablets and stay at home. Pacjent: Dziękuję, panie doktorze. | Thank you, doctor. # 2. W sklepie z odzieżą (in a clothes shop) Sprzedawczyni: Czy mogę w czymś pomóc? | Can I help you? Klient: Szukam kurtki. | I'm looking for a jacket. Sprzedawczyni: Jaki pan ma rozmiar? | What size are you? Klient: Rozmiar M. Czy mogę przymierzyć? | Size M. Can I try it on? Sprzedawczyni: Oczywiście, przymierzalnia jest tam. | Of course, the fitting room is over there. Klient: Ile to kosztuje? | How much is it? Sprzedawczyni: Dwieście złotych. | Two hundred złoty. # 3. W hotelu (at the hotel) Gość: Dobry wieczór, mam rezerwację. | Good evening, I have a reservation. Recepcjonista: Jak się pan nazywa? | What is your name? Gość: Smith. Pokój jednoosobowy na trzy noce. | Smith. A single room for three nights. Recepcjonista: Oto klucz. Śniadanie jest od siódmej do dziesiątej. | Here is your key. Breakfast is from seven to ten. Gość: Czy jest wi-fi? | Is there Wi-Fi? Recepcjonista: Tak, hasło jest na kluczu. | Yes, the password is on the key. # 4. Przez telefon (on the phone) Anna: Halo? | Hello?
