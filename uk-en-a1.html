Section 0How to use this course

Ukrainian is a melodious Slavic language with a Cyrillic alphabet that is easy to learn in a couple of evenings. The spelling is highly phonetic, there are no articles, and the present tense is simple. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, directions, shopping, time and everyday matters.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. Once you know the 33 letters, you can read anything aloud.
  • No articles. книжка = book, the book, a book.
  • Words you know: банк, готель, піца, кава, метро, студент, телефон, футбол, парк.
  • Simple present tense. One form covers "I work" and "I am working", and there is no present-tense "to be".
What will be new. Nouns, adjectives and pronouns change their endings (seven cases), verbs agree with the subject, and the stress can fall anywhere in the word. At A1 you only need the nominative, accusative, locative and a few set patterns. Learn words inside short phrases, not alone.
Main tip. Learn phrases: not "пити = to drink", but "я п'ю каву = I drink coffee". The endings then come by themselves.
Typing answers. The exercises ask for Cyrillic. Add a Ukrainian keyboard on your phone or computer (Settings → Keyboards). You will need it anyway.
False friends. магазин = shop, кабінет = office, study, фабрика = factory, студентка = female student, шляхи = roads, історія = history and story, кіт = cat, рука = hand, arm, хліб = bread, лікар = doctor, чай = tea.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 12.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so rely on the audio. An accent mark over a letter in the Ukrainian words shows the stressed syllable,. "kh" is the Scottish loch, "zh" is the s in measure, and "'" after a letter means it is softened.

Soft consonants

The letters я, ю, є, ї, і, ь make the preceding consonant soft: дя sounds like dya, ть like a soft t'. After most consonants я = ya, ю = yoo, and so on.

Stress. It can fall on any syllable and it often moves between forms (рука́, ру́ки). There is no rule, so learn the stress with the word. In this course the stress is marked with an accent over the vowel in the Ukrainian vocabulary and examples. Unstressed е and и are pulled towards each other, and о before a stressed "у" is close to "u".

3.3. Nominative and accusative

Accusative (the direct object)

Feminine -а → -у, -я → -ю: Я маю книжку. Я бачу жінку. Masculine animate nouns take -а / -я: Я бачу брата. Я маю пса. Masculine inanimate and neuter nouns stay the same: Я маю стіл. Я бачу місто.

Section 4The verbs бути (to be) and мати (to have)

No "to be" in the present

In the present tense Ukrainian simply leaves "to be" out: Я студент. = I am a student. Вона вчителька. = She is a teacher. For "there is / there are" use є: У мене є час. = I have time (lit. "at me there is time"). Past and future use був, була, було, були and буду, будеш… (A2).

Я студе́нт. | ya stoodént | I am a student. Ти втомлений? | ti vtomleny | Are you tired? Ми вдо́ма. | mi vdóma | We are at home. Я маю́ бра́та. | ya mayóo bráta | I have a brother. Ти ма́єш час? | ti máyesh chas | Do you have time? У мене́ нема́є ча́су. | oo mené nemáye chásoo | I have no time.
Negation

Put не before the verb: Я не працюю. Double negatives are normal: Я нічого не маю. (I have nothing.) After a negated verb the object is in the genitive: Я маю час → Я не маю часу. "There isn't" is нема or немає + genitive.

The main verb stays in the infinitive: Я можу прийти. Я хочу пити каву. Я мушу працювати. A learned skill is вміти: Я вмію плавати. "May I" is Можна? and "not allowed" is не можна.

Мо́жна ввійти́? | mózhna vveeytí | May I come in? Я хочу́ пи́ти ка́ву. | ya khochóo píti kávoo | I want to drink coffee. Сього́дні я му́шу працюва́ти. | syohódnee ya móoshoo pratsyoováti | I have to work today. За́раз я не мо́жу розмовля́ти. | záraz ya ne mózhoo rozmovlyáti | I can't talk now. Ти вмієш готува́ти? | ti vmeeyesh hotoováti | Can you cook? Тут не мо́жна кури́ти. | toot ne mózhna kooríti | Smoking is not allowed here.
