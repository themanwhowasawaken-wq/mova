Section 0How to use this course

Spanish is one of the friendliest languages for an English speaker: the spelling is almost perfectly phonetic, the vocabulary overlaps heavily with English, and the grammar is regular. This course takes you to A1: introductions, family, food, directions, shopping, time and everyday matters.

What is on your side

  • Spelling matches sound. Five clean vowels, no silent surprises except the letter h.
  • Thousands of cognates: hotel, hospital, familia, importante, restaurante, teléfono, animal, chocolate.
  • Stress is predictable and an accent mark shows every exception.
  • Regular verbs fall into three groups (-ar, -er, -ir) with predictable endings.
What will be new. Nouns have gender (el libro, la mesa), adjectives agree with them, and verb endings show the person, so subject pronouns are usually dropped. Spanish also has two verbs "to be": ser and estar.
Main tip. Learn phrases, not isolated words: not "beber = to drink", but "bebo agua = I drink water". Gender and endings then come by themselves.
False friends. embarazada = pregnant (not embarrassed), actualmente = currently, librería = bookshop (not library: biblioteca), éxito = success (not exit: salida), constipado = having a cold, sensible = sensitive, realizar = to carry out, asistir = to attend, pretender = to try to, suceso = event.

How to work

  1. Read the explanations and tap words to hear them.
  2. Do the exercises here and on the Exercises page.
  3. 20–30 minutes a day. The plan is in section 12.
About the pronunciation column. It is a simple English-style respelling. It is approximate, so rely on the audio. "kh" is the Scottish loch (Spanish j), "th" is as in think (Spain; in Latin America it is "s"), "ny" is the sound in canyon. Stressed syllables are in capitals only where it helps.

Stress rules (only two!)

1. Words ending in a vowel, -n or -s are stressed on the second-to-last syllable: ha-BLAR, ca-SA, ha-BLAN. 2. Other words are stressed on the last syllable: es-pa-ÑOL, ciu-DAD. 3. Any exception carries an accent mark where the stress falls: teléfono, está, canción.

Questions and exclamations. Spanish opens them with an inverted mark: ¿Cómo estás? ¡Hola! The accent also marks question words: qué, cómo, dónde, cuándo.

Ser or estar?

Ser = identity and lasting qualities: Soy profesor. Es alto. Es de Madrid. Estar = location and temporary state: Estoy en casa. Estoy cansado. ¿Cómo estás?

Soy estudiante. | soy ehstoodyahnteh | I am a student. Estoy cansado. | ehstoy kahnsahdoh | I am tired. Estamos en casa. | ehstahmohs ehn kahsah | We are at home. Tengo un hermano. | tehngoh oon ehrmahnoh | I have a brother. ¿Tienes tiempo? | tyehnehs tyehmpoh | Do you have time? No tengo tiempo. | noh tehngoh tyehmpoh | I have no time.
Negation

Put no before the verb: No soy. No tengo. Double negatives are normal: No tengo nada. (I have nothing.) No viene nadie.

Modal + infinitive

The main verb stays in the infinitive: ¿Puedo entrar? Quiero beber un café. "Have to" is tener que + infinitive: Tengo que trabajar. A learned skill is saber: Sé nadar.
